## Figure label placement

Five rules, all inferred from the label positions Nico corrected by hand in
the Problem Set 1 solutions (2026-09-08). They are enforced in
`_ps_theme.py`, so a new figure should get them for free — the rules are
here so a future change to the helpers does not undo them.

1. **No curve may run through a label.** If a curve would cross its own
   label, the label moves into the clear band between curves — never the
   other way around.
2. **A curve label belongs just above its own curve's lower end.** Not on a
   shared horizontal line with the other curves' labels: on a fan of four
   demand curves, each label sits at its own curve's height, which is what
   tells the reader which curve is which.
3. **An equilibrium label sits centred directly above its dot.** No
   alternating left / right offsets. (`Panel.equilibrium` defaults to
   `lbl_align="c"`, `dy=-0.30`.)
4. **An annotation arrow lives inside the band between the two curves it
   relates** — a shift arrow sits between `D` and `D′`, not outside them.
5. **The figure box is cropped tight to the ink, with ZERO padding.**
   `place(f, doc, ...)` sets the group's `off` / `ext` / `chOff` / `chExt`
   to `content_box(f)` at 1:1 scale, so nothing inside moves or stretches.
   Dead margin pushes figures apart and floats the captions; 0.02" of pad
   per side added 0.040" per figure and accumulated into a seventh page in
   the PS 1 solutions.

**Apply the alignment shift once.** `Fig.label` already shifts x for a
non-left `align`, so `Panel.tick_y` / `tick_x` / `title` / `text` must pass
the RAW anchor. Pre-shifting in the caller and passing an alignment too
shifts twice — that is how every tick in the PS 1 solutions sat about a
quarter inch off its axis.

### Every label goes through the helper that owns its rule

A label placed by a bare `Panel.text()` is outside the rules above **by
construction** — `text()` puts a box at a coordinate and knows nothing about
curves or dots. So:

- a **curve** label goes through `curve()` / `demand()` / `supply()`, which
  anchors it at the curve's own lower end and takes `lbl_dx` / `lbl_dy`;
- an **equilibrium** label goes through `equilibrium(label=…)`, which
  centres it just above the dot;
- `text()` is for free annotations only ("Higher income shifts …"), never
  for a label one of the rules covers.

**Never hard-code a coordinate Nico dragged to.** A literal anchor is a
snapshot of one figure at one moment; it cannot follow the rule the drag was
expressing, and it silently outranks the rule on the next rebuild. Port a
hand-placed label by finding the **rule-based offset that reproduces it** —
`lbl_dx=-0.17, lbl_dy=-0.31` on the curve's own label, not
`text(63.53, 23.64, …)` — so the label still lands correctly when the
numbers behind the figure change.

This is exactly how the 2026-09-08 round was missed. Round 1's positions
went in as literal `text()` anchors; the rules were then written down and
believed to be enforced "in the helpers", which was true and vacuous — the
two figures in question never called those helpers. The `D`ₐ label stayed
mid-curve and the four 3(b) equilibrium labels stayed **down-right** of
their dots, both contradicting rules already stated, until Nico had to move
all five by hand a second time.

**Close a port by auditing against the RULES, not only against his file.**
Comparing a render to his previous version can only find where I differ from
him — never where we both differ from a rule just stated. After inferring or
changing a rule, re-derive every label the rule covers and list any that the
build does not place that way.

**Tolerance:** a rule-derived position within about **0.07"** of a hand-drag
is a match — that is the scatter of dragging a box with a mouse. Keep the
rule's position, not the hand position, once inside that band. A deviation of
0.4" is not scatter; it means the label was never under the rule.

**The rules are about GLYPHS, not label boxes.** A label box is bigger than
its text — `label()` adds 0.08" of width slack (0.04 a side) and sets a
0.22" box around ~0.152" of 11 pt cap height (0.034 a side). Nico's own
accepted positions let a curve cross that padding, so a box-level test
reports collisions that are not there, and a box-level clearance test
rejects placements that in fact fit. Measure the padded-in rectangle.

**Which end of a curve gets the label.** `curve(..., lbl_end=1)` (the
default) labels `p1`, the lower end of a downward-sloping curve, per rule 2.
`lbl_end=0` labels `p0` instead, for a curve whose lower end is too crowded
— a reaction function in q₁–q₂ space, where a label beside the line is cut
through by it, takes its label above the vertical intercept. Rule 1 outranks
rule 2 when they conflict; the anchor still comes from the curve.

**A label inside a shaded region is placed by search, not by a fraction.**
Where a curve crosses the region — MC always crosses the profit rectangle,
since the optimum is where MC meets the price line — scan the region for the
(quantity, height) with the most clearance from every curve, and fall back
to the short `π > 0` / `π < 0` form only when nothing fits. A hand-picked
fraction of `Q*` silently stops working when the cost functions change.
Reference: `_clear_spot` in `_build_PS3_Solutions.py`.

### The audit script

`ruleaudit.py` (in the session scratchpad) checks a built `.docx` against
rules 1 and 3 mechanically: it reconstructs every curve from
`prstGeom="line"` (endpoints from the bbox plus flipH/flipV) and from
`custGeom` point paths, then reports a curve crossing a label's glyph area
and any two label glyph areas overlapping. Guides, axes, ticks and legend
labels are excluded — a dashed guide is meant to reach its label, and a
legend label sits on an opaque white badge drawn after the gridlines. Run it
over all ten documents at the end of any figure work; it should print zero.

Everything in the Teaching `CLAUDE.md` figure section still holds: a demand
curve is dark red `C00000` (curve and label alike), supply is navy, every
label sits in a box measured to its own text in the real font, axis titles
are anchored to the arrow tip, and marked points must lie mathematically on
the curves they annotate.

## Change-based table rows

**A change belongs BETWEEN the two rows it is derived from** (2026-09-09,
Nico — the convention from the slides). A marginal product, a marginal
cost, a marginal revenue is a statement about the STEP from one row to the
next, not about either row on its own. Module 3 slide 19 does this
literally: the ΔQ / ΔL / MPL columns of the MPL table are left EMPTY and
each value is a floating box centred on the border between the two rows it
comes from, with an arrow spanning them.

**Use `S.stagger_table`: real merged cells, two half-rows per level.**
Every level gets two table rows. The level columns are merged across the
pair, so their number sits centred in the level. The change columns are
merged across a pair shifted one half-row DOWN, so each change value
centres on the border between the two levels it comes from. The stagger
is then real geometry, not a nudge — and it is visible even when the cells
are empty, which is what the blank student table needs.

`rows` is one list per level, each change value on the level its step
starts from (the last level's change cells are blank). `change_cols` names
the columns to stagger. `highlight` takes LEVEL indices, not row indices.
Numbers are centred; the caller sizes the columns.

Two earlier mechanisms, both replaced:

- a thin cream row woven in between every pair of level rows — read
  correctly but doubled the horizontal rules, and Nico's verdict was
  "really ugly";
- a paragraph `space_before` pushing the change columns' text down half a
  row — no extra rules, but the offset was invisible in the blank table
  and the value still sat inside its own bordered cell.

**The change columns sit at the FAR RIGHT of the table** (2026-09-13,
Nico: "always move the change columns to the very right"). The Bats tables
run `Output … ATC, TR, Profit, MC, MR`; before this the two staggered
columns were interleaved with the level columns. **The blank student table
and the key must match** — a student fills one in and checks it against the
other — so `_build_PS3.py` and `_build_PS3_Solutions.py` carry the same
order.

**Carry the column order in the HEADER LIST, never in indices.** Both files
used to address cells as `row[4]` / `row[8]` / `row[10]` beside `I_MC` /
`I_MR`, so moving a column would have put values silently under the wrong
headers. Rows are now built into a dict keyed by column NAME and emitted as
`[v.get(h, "") for h in BATS_HEAD]`, with widths from a `BATS_WIDTH` dict.
Reordering the header is then the whole change.

**Each merged change cell is filled with a vertical FADE**, from the colour
of the level the change is derived FROM to the colour of the level it runs
TO (2026-09-13, Nico) — so the cell shows which two rows made it. A rule
above and below it, as before. The two leftover half-boxes at top and bottom
stay EMPTY: there is no step above the first level or below the last.

### The fade: a table cell has no gradient, so it is a FLOATING shape

This took two attempts and the first diagnosis was half wrong, so both
halves are worth keeping.

**True:** Word has no gradient for a table cell. `w:shd` is a solid fill plus
an optional dither pattern and `CT_Shd` has no gradient element, so a fade
has to be a shape.

**What was wrong:** the first attempt concluded the shape could never cover
the cell. It used `wp:inline` (`_gradient_box`), and an INLINE drawing sits
on a text line — exact line spacing clips whatever exceeds the line and
automatic spacing grows the row. That is a property of inline drawings, not
of shapes.

**`wp:anchor` is not on a text line at all.** Positioned absolutely, with
`behindDoc="1"` and `layoutInCell="1"`, it takes no part in line layout and
can be sized to the whole cell. The number goes back to being ordinary cell
text, painted on top. Reference: `_grad_anchor` in `_ps_theme.py`, reached
by `stagger_table(..., change_fade=True)`.

Three things are load-bearing, and each one failed visibly on the way:

1. **The run carrying the anchor must come AFTER `w:pPr`.** `w:pPr` has to
   be the first child of `w:p`. Inserting the run at index 0 pushes it down,
   Word then ignores the paragraph properties entirely — the numbers lost
   their centring and nothing painted at all.
2. **The cell must be shaded `auto`, not white.** A `behindDoc` shape is
   painted BEHIND the table's own cell shading, so an opaque `w:shd` hides
   the fade completely.
3. **The horizontal offset is minus the LEFT CELL MARGIN**, which this
   module's `_set_cell_margins` sets to `side_margin` flat. `T._cell_margins`
   adds 0.04" to the sides; borrowing that number put the shape 2.88 pt left
   of the cell, where Word CLIPPED it — so it fell exactly that far short on
   the right, which looks like the coverage bug all over again.

**Measure it, do not look at it.** A white-to-cream fade is half invisible
to a pixel test. Repaint the gradient stops red→blue in a copy of the built
file, export, and sample the raster: the fade must span the cell's borders
on all four sides. Current reading: **361.68–444.93 pt against borders at
361.80–444.60**, and contiguous 26.5 pt blocks against a 26.10 pt cell.

`_gradient_box` keeps the old inline route for reference only; do not use
it. `change_rules=False` drops the interior rules for one continuous strip.

Consequence to know: a highlighted level is pale gold across the level
columns only, but a fade that touches it runs gold→white, which is the
point — the change cell names the two rows it came from.




### Four traps, all of them things python-docx cannot see

A vertically merged region is ONE `_Cell` in python-docx, mapped to the
RESTART cell. Anything written through `tbl.cell(r, c)` therefore lands on
the restart row, and the continue rows keep whatever they had. Three of
these four bugs are that one fact:

1. **Merge BEFORE writing text.** `merge()` concatenates the paragraphs of
   both cells, and a second paragraph is a second line, which a half-height
   row has no room for.
2. **Set the paragraph MARK's size** (`_pmark_size`). A line box is as tall
   as the tallest thing on it and the mark counts; left at the style's
   11 pt every row lays out at 0.186" whatever the runs say. An empty cell
   has nothing *but* its mark.
3. **Flatten the continue cells** (`_flatten_continue_cells`). They are
   invisible but they still SET THE ROW HEIGHT: at the Normal style's
   11 pt mark plus its space-after they are ~0.30" tall, and with
   `hRule="atLeast"` every row grows to fit them. The Bats table measured
   **7.74"** against the 5.0" its declared half-rows come to.
4. **Keep-together needs the raw XML** (`_keep_together_merged`).
   `T._keep_table_together` walks `row.cells`, so the continue cells' own
   paragraphs never get `w:keepNext` — 198 of 385 flagged on the Bats
   table — and every unflagged one is a page-break opportunity. Both cost
   tables broke across two pages until this was fixed.

Also: `w:tcPr`'s children are ORDERED, and `w:tcBorders` must precede
`w:shd`. `_tcpr_put` places every hand-built element at its schema
position; `T._shade` and `T._cell_margins` APPEND, which is why this
module has its own `_shade` and `_set_cell_margins`. Verify with an
order check over every cell — it should be zero.

**Row height comes from the FONT.** `half_row_h(size)` is one Calibri line,
`1.22 × size / 72` plus a little slack — 0.147" at 8 pt, 0.181" at 10 pt.
The 1.22 matters: using the point size itself is what first sized these
rows at 0.24" and would have clipped the values. `hRule="atLeast"` so Word
grows a row rather than hiding anything.

Two conventions apply and they are easy to conflate:

- **How the change is computed** — Module 3 slide 46, "Convention: use the
  next-higher input level (Δ goes forward)": a forward difference.
- **Where it is PLOTTED** — at the **MIDPOINT of its interval** (2026-09-13,
  Nico). It used to be the initial point; the midpoint makes the figure say
  what the staggered table says, that the value belongs to the STEP and not
  to either level. So the tulip MP figure plots at 0.5, 1.5 … 7.5, and its x
  ticks run 0 – 8, the fertilizer LEVELS, so each point visibly falls
  between two of them. The question in `_build_PS3.py` tells students the
  same thing, and it has to keep doing so.
  - Widening the tick range put the last tick at the right-hand end of the
    plot, exactly where the axis title is anchored to the arrow tip, and it
    printed on top of "Fertilizer". The x scale is `t / 8.8` rather than
    `t / 8.0` to keep them apart. `_ruleaudit` excludes tick labels, so only
    the render catches this.
- **Where it is written in a table** — on the boundary, as above.

The old problem-set wording ("enter it at the initial point") conflated
them, so its table half was removed and its plotting half restated.

**Students get the convention too, in the same words everywhere.**
`S.change_note(doc)` emits `CHANGE_CONVENTION` as the house cream aside in
every problem that asks for a change-based series. Its second half carries
as much weight as the first: a student working in Excel cannot merge
half-rows, so the note tells them to label each change with the interval
it refers to instead ("MC, 0 → 1,000"). The point of the convention is
that the reader can tell which step each number belongs to.

`change_table` (the space_before version) is still in `_ps_theme.py` and
unused. Delete it once the stagger has been seen on paper.

## Build mechanics

- **Save through `S.save(doc, path)`, never `doc.save(path)`.**
  python-docx's template declares Word's 2010 layout engine
  (`compatibilityMode 14`); Word 2013+ breaks lines differently, so a
  document saved at 14 wraps identical text one line longer than the same
  file after Nico opens and saves it. `S.save` patches
  `word/settings.xml` to mode 15 after saving. Mode 15 also renders faster.
- **Rendering to PDF: one document per PowerShell process.** Looping
  several `ExportAsFixedFormat` calls inside one process hangs Word
  indefinitely (CPU burning, `Responding` True, no output). Kill leftover
  `WINWORD` processes between runs. And never name a PowerShell parameter
  `$Doc` while using `$doc` for the Document object — PowerShell variables
  are case-insensitive, so the parameter shadows it and every method call
  fails with "[System.String] does not contain a method named …".
- **Don't render PDFs unless asked.** Nico exports them himself. This is
  about DELIVERABLES: exporting to the session scratchpad to measure a
  layout is a different thing and is expected — see *Pagination* below.
  Never write a `.pdf` into the project folder without being asked.
- **A `~$` lock file does NOT prove Nico has the document open.** Every
  Word COM run leaves one behind when the instance is force-killed or
  fails to quit cleanly, so the folder accumulates stale owner files from
  my own measurement passes. The Teaching `CLAUDE.md` rule ("a lock file
  means I have that deck open — do NOT kill Word") therefore cannot be
  applied to the lock file alone. **Test for a VISIBLE window instead:**
  `Get-Process WINWORD | Select Id, MainWindowTitle` — a non-empty
  `MainWindowTitle` is his; a blank one is an orphaned COM instance.
  Delete a stale lock only after confirming no visible window owns that
  document. (2026-09-10: acting on the lock file alone, I force-killed
  Word while a solutions lock was present and later deleted the owner
  file of a Problem Set 1 window he had open on screen.)
- **Bash heredocs lose one backslash level on this machine.** Any patch
  script whose match strings contain backslashes must be written to a `.py`
  file with the Write tool and run by path.

## Pagination: measure CLEARANCE, never page count

A page count looks fine right up until it flips, so it cannot tell you
whether a document is one wrapped line away from spilling. The number that
can is the **bottom clearance of each page** — how far the last body line
sits above the bottom margin. Measure it off the EXPORTED PDF, not off Word:

```python
# after ExportAsFixedFormat(..., 17)
bl = [b for b in page.get_text("blocks") if b[4].strip() and b[3] <= 725]
free = 720 - max(b[3] for b in bl)      # 720 = 792 - 72 bottom margin
```

The `y1 <= 725` filter drops the footer, which sits inside the bottom
margin and otherwise reports every page as overflowing by 36 pt. A page
that ends early (end of document, a forced break) reads in the hundreds; a
full page reads 0–17 pt, which is normal. What matters is the **last page's**
tail room, because that is the buffer before a new page appears.

### A page's clearance is a SHOCK ABSORBER — do not spend all of it

2026-09-12, the worked case. Adding the navy `submit_card` to PS 1 page 1
left that page with **3.1 pt** of clearance, and the last thing on it was
Problem 2(c), a **two-line** paragraph. Widow/orphan control makes a
two-line paragraph all-or-nothing, so a renderer laying out a few points
taller dropped the whole **35 pt** block onto page 2 — which had only
41.6 pt of tail room, so Problem 4(c) was pushed to a third page. Nico saw
it on Save-as-PDF; this machine never reproduced it.

So the failure needs no bug at all, only two thin margins in a row. After
adding ANY block to a page — a card, a figure, a table — re-measure both
numbers: the clearance on the page you added to, and the tail room on the
last page. A two-line paragraph sitting at a page foot is the specific
shape to look for, since it migrates in one 35 pt lump rather than a line
at a time.

### The slack is in the paragraph spacing

These documents carry about **4.5 inches** of pure paragraph spacing, so
there is always room to buy margin without touching content or his
hand-sized cards. Current values, tightened on 2026-09-12 from the figures
in brackets — they bought PS 1 page 1 3.1 → 22 pt and page 2 41.6 → 67 pt,
with **no page count changing anywhere**:

| helper | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `problem()` | 12 (was 17) | 4 (was 6) |
| `part()` | 6 (was 8) | 3 (was 4) |
| `draws_on()` (duplicated in all ten build scripts) | 0 | 5 (was 8) |
| `submit_card()` | 5 (was 6) | 0 (was 2) |

Re-measure all ten documents after any change here: the spacing helpers are
shared, so a trim made for one problem set repaginates the other nine.

### Things that are NOT the cause — do not chase them

- **Tracked changes.** Markup view is longer than clean text, so it is worth
  ONE check (`w:ins` / `w:del` count in `document.xml`), and then dropping.
- **Printer metrics.** `<w:usePrinterMetrics/>` is absent from these files,
  which is the safe state: Word lays out from document metrics, not from
  whichever printer is selected. Absent = the "use printer metrics" box is
  unchecked. Verify once, then stop.
- **Word’s own page count**, via COM or otherwise. It said 2 pages through
  every variation — hidden and visible, read-only and read-write — while he
  was looking at 3. `ComputeStatistics(2)` is the measurement that was
  wrong; the paragraph map (`Range.Information(3)` and `(6)` for page and y,
  paragraph by paragraph) is the one that found the answer. This is the
  "a repeated instruction means the MEASUREMENT is wrong" rule under
  *Verifying a hand-edit port*, and it cost two rounds to remember.

### `cream_card` measures every card in BOLD ITALIC

`T.style_run` writes `<w:b w:val="0"/>` and `<w:i w:val="0"/>` on every plain
run, and `_measure_par` tests for the ELEMENT rather than its value — so it
sizes every card as if the text were bold italic Calibri. That is about 4 %
wide, which is enough to report two lines for a sentence Word sets on one.
It is what first made `submit_card` 0.55" tall instead of 0.34" and put PS 1
on three pages.

`_tn_theme.py` is the shared engine and is not to be modified, so the fix is
the one `policy_card` already used: **pass an explicit `height_in`**. Compute
it as `1.22 × size / 72` per line plus the shape’s 0.12" of top and bottom
inset, then confirm against the rendered shape — Word COM reports it as
`Document.Shapes.Item(i).Height` in points, and these cards do NOT appear in
`InlineShapes`.

## Tracked changes

Changes that go beyond formatting are proposed as Word revisions
(`w:ins` / `w:del`, author "Claude (proposed)") so Nico can accept or reject
them in the review pane. Two things to know:

- **Revisions inside a text box never reach the review pane.** Keep
  proposals in ordinary body paragraphs.
- **Once he accepts a round, strip the markup from the build script** — the
  accepted text becomes the plain text of the script, and the docstring
  records what changed and when. A document he has accepted should report
  zero revisions on a fresh build.

## Verifying a hand-edit port

He edits the `.docx` in Word; his version is preserved as the `_t-1` file.
Close the port with measurements, not with a visual read:

- Diff the figure geometry shape by shape at rendered inches, removing the
  uniform crop shift first, and account for every residual.
- Compare `<wp:extent>` on every inline drawing — his boxes are the ink
  bounding box, so a mismatch means the crop is wrong.
- Compare page counts and the first / last line of each page against a
  render of his file. A pagination difference is a real difference, not a
  rounding artifact.
- **A repeated instruction means the measurement is wrong, not the edit.**
  If the same correction has to be pointed out twice, stop and re-measure
  with a different tool before touching the build script.
