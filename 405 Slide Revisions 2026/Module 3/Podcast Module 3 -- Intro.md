## What Module 3 will cover (preview material — high level, no answers)

Module 3 tackles two of the most basic questions any business faces: how do we turn inputs into output, and what does it all cost? The class calls these two halves **Production** and **Costs**. The point is never theory for its own sake — it's how these ideas drive real management decisions about hiring, investment, pricing, and growth.

On the **production** side, the class will start from a deceptively simple question: how many people should a company actually hire? Building toward an answer, we'll meet the idea of **diminishing returns** — why the tenth hire and the thousandth hire are not worth the same to a firm. Then we'll draw a sharp line between the **short run**, where you're stuck with the factory and equipment you already have, and the **long run**, where you get to choose the whole mix of people and machines from scratch — two situations that lead to genuinely different decisions. Expect a running electric-vehicle example to anchor it, a striking story from centuries ago, and a very current one straight out of the AI talent wars.

On the **costs** side, the class will sort out which costs actually matter for a decision — and which ones quietly lead even very smart people astray. There's one particular kind of cost that feels like it should count but, once it's spent, ought to be ignored completely; we'll see why through a famous Hollywood flop and some very large recent bets in the tech world. We'll also separate the cost of *one more* unit from the *average* cost, and see why mixing up the two produces bad calls on everything from gym packages to loans to factory output.

Finally, the class will ask why bigger is sometimes cheaper and sometimes not — the ideas of **economies of scale** and **economies of scope** — with examples that will range from airplanes to Amazon.

Here's the thread to listen for. Underneath all of these decisions is one simple way of thinking that keeps coming back, again and again. We won't give it away here — but once the class puts a name to it, you'll start seeing it behind nearly every business decision you make. Come ready to dig in.
