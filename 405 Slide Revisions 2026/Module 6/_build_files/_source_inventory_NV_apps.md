The dilemma of pricing is that if you charge a high price to capture a lot of value, you exclude many from wanting to buy the product, namely the hordes of those with valuations for the product that are lower than price. The alternative is to go with volume, but then you have to charge a low price and you give away a lot of consumer surplus, leave a lot of surplus on the table which you do not capture. There is a dilemma. Today we will see how to escape the dilemma through price discrimination.

**NOTES:** Another Example: Fun Film sold at Price Costco. This is a classic example. Kodak was selling their premium Royal Gold film. Fuji and others started competing. Kodak wanted to protect the low end of their market. They could have lowered the price on Royal Gold, but then you give up capturing the surplus at the high end. So they introduced a new brand, FunFilm, offered only at discount stores like Sam’s Club, Costco, in bulk. This is where price sensitive customers go. It turns out the two films were chemically identical. The packaging and bulk sales aspect were different but the actual product was the same. This is a classic B-school case.
