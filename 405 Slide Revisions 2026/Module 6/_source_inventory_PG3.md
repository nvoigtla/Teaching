**NOTES:** An important distinction is between private value auctions and common value auctions: 

In a private value auction, each bidder has a private value for an item (such as a Picasso painting) and this value is known to the bidder. Other bidders’ valuations for the item, however, are not necessarily known, and different bidders may have different valuations for the item. Each bidder must form expectations on what the other bidders’ valuations and bids are going to be. 
 
In a common value auction, in contrast, bidders would all share the same valuation for the item, if that value were known. Unfortunately, in a common value auction the value of the item is not known to any of the bidders. Only the distribution of possible valuations is known. For instance, when bidding for an oil field, you might have some idea of the oil reserves it contains, and thus the potential value of exploiting the oil field, but there is uncertainty on that value. Everyone would agree on the value, if it were known, but it is not known. Each bidder must form expectations on the true value of the object, and on the beliefs of other bidders.

**NOTES:** We will consider 4 major types of auctions:
 
The English Auction. Also known as ascending auction. Under this form of auction bidders orally announce their bids in increasing order, and the last bidder to offer a bid obtains the item. Christies and Sotheby’s conduct art auctions like this. 

The Dutch Auction. Also known as descending auction. The auctioneer starts at a high price and reduces it by given increments. The first bidder to bid wins the item. Flowers in Aalsmeer, Holland, the world’s largest flower market, are sold in this way.

The Sealed-Bid Auction. Bidders submit sealed bids, not knowing what other players have bid. The highest bid wins and pays the amount offered. Real-estate is often sold in this way.

The Second-Price Auction. Also known as the Vickrey Auction (after the economics Nobel Prize winner who studied them, James Vickrey). In this auction format, bidders submit sealed bids. The best bid wins, but the winner pays the amount offered by the second highest bidder. Slots for advertisers in Google’s search results pages are sold using this auction format.
 
There are many other types (e.g., all-pay auction).
