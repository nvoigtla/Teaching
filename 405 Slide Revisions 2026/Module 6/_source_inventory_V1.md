**NOTES:** When an individual can buy several units of an item, the seller can think about a volume pricing strategy. This is the case of continuous choice as opposed to binary choice (buy/don’t buy). Diminishing marginal personal value comes into play – the value of each subsequent unit falls. 
 
We will talk about 3 kinds of volume pricing; we will see examples of each - they all amount to the same thing:
— Fixed fee + access price
— All or nothing pricing: bundle.
— Quantity discounts: sliding scale. 
Examples of volume pricing:
— Rockreation: fixed monthly fee, unlimited access. Also, price differences with SLC. Is it price discrimination (probably, no – may reflect cost differentials, especially rent)
— Health Club: Annual fee
— Car Wash: Fixed fee - unlimited washes 
— Netflix: Monthly fee – “unlimited” rentals

Analytics will help to clarify all this here. We still abstract from cost and set MC=0. The optimal price is where Q=10 because the slope of MR is twice the slope of D and we are setting MR=0. Under simple pricing, there is unexploited volume equal to the lower triangle ($100 of value) and the consumer retains a surplus of $100. The company ends up with revenues of $200. There is some opportunity to do some business here.

**NOTES:** Ask in class if this is a price searcher or price taker

The dilemma of pricing is that if you charge a high price to capture a lot of value, you exclude many from wanting to buy the product, namely the hordes of those with valuations for the product that are lower than price. The alternative is to go with volume, but then you have to charge a low price and you give away a lot of consumer surplus, leave a lot of surplus on the table which you do not capture. There is a dilemma. Today we will see how to escape the dilemma through price discrimination.

Firms are not better off, since only selling at one price, the lower price!

- TXT [0.23,0.14 9.54x1.25]: Three degrees of price discrimination
- TXT [0.62,1.59 8.76x5.07]: First degree: ⏎ “Dream world”: firm knows each customer’s willingness to pay (marginal personal value – MPV) ⏎ Third degree: ⏎ Firm can identify specific groups of customers with different MPV (students, seniors, tourists) ⏎ Second degree (“indirect”): ⏎ Firm cannot directly identify the different customers’ MPV. Instead, the firm uses different purchase options to infer willingness to pay: versioning, quantity discounts, coupons
